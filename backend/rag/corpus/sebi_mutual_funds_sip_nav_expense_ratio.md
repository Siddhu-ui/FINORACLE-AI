## Mutual Funds Overview
A mutual fund is a SEBI-regulated investment vehicle that pools money from multiple investors with a common financial objective and invests the corpus across diversified securities such as equities, government bonds, corporate debt, or money-market instruments. Each investor owns units proportional to their investment. Mutual funds in India are structured as trusts managed by an Asset Management Company (AMC) overseen by Trustees and regulated under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations.

## Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)
A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows investors to invest a fixed rupee amount at regular intervals (typically monthly) into a chosen mutual fund scheme rather than investing a lump sum at once. SIPs encourage financial discipline and utilize rupee cost averaging: when market prices fall, the fixed SIP amount buys more units; when prices rise, it buys fewer units. Over long investment horizons, SIPs reduce market-timing risk and allow returns to compound steadily.

## Net Asset Value (NAV) and AUM
Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the per-unit market value of a mutual fund scheme. It is calculated at the end of each business day as: (Total Market Value of Scheme Assets minus Liabilities and Expenses) divided by Total Outstanding Units. A lower NAV does not make a fund cheaper or better; returns depend on percentage growth in NAV. Assets Under Management (AUM) is the total market value of all securities held by the mutual fund scheme on behalf of its investors.

## Expense Ratio (TER) and Exit Load
Total Expense Ratio (TER) is the annual percentage fee charged by an AMC to cover fund management, registrar, custodial, audit, and distribution costs. SEBI prescribes strict maximum TER slabs that decrease as a scheme's AUM grows. Direct plans have lower expense ratios than Regular plans because they exclude distributor commissions. Exit Load is a small percentage fee (commonly 1% if equity units are redeemed within 12 months) charged on early redemption to discourage short-term outflows and protect remaining investors.
