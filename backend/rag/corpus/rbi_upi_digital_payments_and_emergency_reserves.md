## Emergency Fund and Liquidity Reserves
RBI financial literacy guidelines emphasize maintaining a dedicated emergency fund covering 3 to 6 months of essential household expenses (rent, groceries, utility bills, school fees, insurance premiums, and mandatory loan EMIs) before taking market risk. Emergency reserves should prioritize instant liquidity and capital safety—such as savings bank accounts, sweep-in fixed deposits, or overnight/liquid funds—rather than volatile equities or locked-in instruments.

## UPI and Safe Digital Banking Practices
Unified Payments Interface (UPI), operated by NPCI under RBI oversight, enables instant 24x7 inter-bank transfers using virtual payment addresses. RBI advisories instruct citizens never to share UPI PINs, OTPs, or CVV codes, remembering that a UPI PIN is required only to send money—never to receive funds. Regularly auditing bank and UPI statements helps detect unusual spending or unauthorized debits early; unauthorized electronic banking transactions should be reported to the bank promptly to limit customer liability under RBI's zero-liability circular.

## Loan EMIs, Interest Costs, and Prepayment
An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) combines both interest and principal repayment. In early loan years, a larger share of each EMI services interest, while principal amortization accelerates later. Under RBI directives, banks and NBFCs cannot levy foreclosure charges or prepayment penalties on floating-rate term loans sanctioned to individual borrowers for non-business purposes. Making periodic principal prepayments or increasing EMIs reduces total interest outgo and shortens loan tenure. Total household EMIs should ideally remain below 35% to 40% of net monthly take-home income.
