## Budgeting and Cash Flow Discipline
Personal budgeting begins by tracking actual inflows and outflows rather than relying on estimates. A practical benchmark promoted in financial education is the 50/30/20 allocation rule: up to 50% of post-tax income toward essential needs (housing, groceries, utilities, healthcare, mandatory EMIs), up to 30% toward discretionary wants (dining, entertainment, lifestyle shopping), and at least 20% toward savings, emergency buffers, and goal-oriented investments. Adopting a pay-yourself-first approach—automating SIPs or recurring savings right after salary credit—improves long-term consistency.

## Impact of Inflation on Purchasing Power
Inflation is the persistent rise in the general price level of goods and services, which erodes the purchasing power of money over time. At 6% annual inflation, an expense of ₹50,000 per month today will require roughly ₹89,542 per month after 10 years and ₹1,60,357 per month after 20 years to maintain the same standard of living. Real return equals nominal investment return adjusted for inflation and taxes; keeping long-term wealth solely in low-interest savings accounts often produces negative real returns.

## Power of Compound Interest and Goal Horizons
Compound interest generates returns not only on the original principal invested, but also on previously accumulated interest or gains. Starting early dramatically reduces the required monthly investment for long-term goals like retirement or higher education. The Rule of 72 gives a quick approximation of how many years an investment takes to double: divide 72 by the annual percentage rate of return (for example, at 12% annualized return, capital doubles in approximately 6 years). Matching asset choice to goal horizon is essential: short-term goals (<3 years) favor debt/FDs, while long-term goals (>5–7 years) can allocate to diversified equity funds.
