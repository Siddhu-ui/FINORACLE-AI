## Equity, Debt, Hybrid, and Index Funds
Under SEBI's scheme categorization adopted across AMFI member fund houses, Equity Mutual Funds invest predominantly (at least 65%) in company shares such as Large-Cap, Mid-Cap, Small-Cap, or Flexi-Cap portfolios, targeting long-term inflation-beating wealth creation with higher short-term volatility. Debt Mutual Funds invest in fixed-income instruments like Treasury Bills, Government Securities (G-Secs), Commercial Paper, and Corporate Bonds, offering lower volatility and suitability for short-to-medium horizons or emergency reserves (Liquid and Ultra-Short Duration funds). Index Funds are passively managed schemes that replicate a market benchmark such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex at a significantly lower expense ratio than actively managed funds.

## Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)
An Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) is a diversified equity mutual fund with a mandatory statutory lock-in period of 3 years from the date of each unit allotment (for SIPs, each monthly installment is locked in for 3 years individually). Investments in ELSS qualify for income tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income-tax Act up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year under the Old Tax Regime, making it the shortest lock-in tax-saving investment among Section 80C options.

## Diversification and Riskometer
Diversification spreads investments across asset classes (equity, debt, gold), market capitalizations, and sectors so that poor performance in any single security or industry does not derail the entire portfolio. Every mutual fund scheme in India displays a mandatory six-level SEBI Riskometer (Low, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderately High, High, and Very High) so investors can match scheme risk with their financial goal horizon and risk tolerance.
