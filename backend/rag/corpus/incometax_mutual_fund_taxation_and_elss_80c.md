## Equity Mutual Fund Capital Gains Taxation
In India, taxation of mutual fund redemptions depends on the asset category and holding period. For equity-oriented mutual funds (investing at least 65% in domestic equities), gains on units held for 12 months or less are treated as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) under Section 111A (taxed at 20% for transfers on or after 23 July 2024, previously 15%). Gains on equity mutual fund units held for more than 12 months qualify as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) under Section 112A, with an annual tax-free exemption limit of ₹1,25,000 per financial year and gains above the threshold taxed at 12.5% without indexation (for transfers on or after 23 July 2024).

## Debt Mutual Fund Taxation
For specified mutual funds (such as debt funds investing less than 35% in domestic equities) acquired on or after 1 April 2023 under Section 50AA, capital gains are treated as short-term capital gains regardless of holding period and taxed at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate. Dividend income (IDCW payouts) from mutual funds is added to the investor's taxable income and taxed at applicable slab rates, subject to TDS above statutory thresholds.

## Old vs New Tax Regime and Section 80C
Individual taxpayers in India can choose between the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), which offers lower slab rates and a standard deduction for salaried individuals with fewer exemptions, and the Old Tax Regime, which allows deductions such as Section 80C (up to ₹1,50,000 across ELSS, PPF, EPF, NSC, life insurance premium, and home loan principal), Section 80D (health insurance premiums), HRA exemption, and home loan interest under Section 24(b). Taxpayers should compare actual tax liability under both regimes each financial year and verify current Finance Act rates before filing.
